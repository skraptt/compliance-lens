# Legal accuracy review: REMIT checklist v0.1 (remit-v0.1-under-review.yaml)

Reviewed: 2026-11-17. All sources were checked with the REMIT MCP. Its sources were last checked 2026-11-17; the texts were fetched 2026-10-30, and the ACER Exposure Guidance on 2026-11-03. I did not edit the YAML.
Abbreviations: G6.1 = ACER Guidance on the application of REMIT, 6.1st ed. (18 Dec 2024); IR 2026 = Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/256; IR 2014 = Implementing Regulation (EU) No 1348/2014; OL = ACER Open Letter.

## Verdict

The checklist is broadly sound. Every article number I checked exists, and the core deadlines in the requirements are correct: T+2/T+10 since 29 Apr 2026, exposure reporting from 1 Jan 2027 with the first report by 31 Oct 2027, the four-week STOR deadline, and five-year algo records. Every guidance section it cites exists and says roughly what is claimed. There are no wrong deadlines. The REP-02 note does need one date correction (LNG). The problems are of four other kinds:
- One addressee is too broad (DIS-05).
- A **wrong scoping statement** in STR-01: market participants that provide DEA, or that provide order books, are PPATs and so do have Art. 15 duties.
- Two reporting items miss duties under Article 8 IR 2026 (REP-01/REP-03), and the REP-02 note misses a staggered application date (LNG, Art. 10(6), applies only from 29 Oct 2027).
- A number of `procedure_should_cover` items, and some `requirement` wording, go beyond the law or the guidance. Examples are the kill switch, NDAs, banning disclaimers, "must" for forecasts, periodic registration review, and "ACER standard format" in DIS-04. These would generate false gaps. They should be labelled **good practice, not required**, or reworded as ACER expectations.

The two `binding: guidance` labels you asked about are right for the one-hour target (DIS-02) and the UMM format (DIS-03). In both items, though, the underlying duty (to disclose effectively and on time) is law. Several `binding: law` items also contain checks that come only from ACER guidance (INS-02, DIS-04, STR-03).

## Answers to the open questions

**(a) REMIT-DIS-02: is "as soon as possible, at the latest within one hour" guidance only?**
Yes. Art. 4(1) REMIT only requires disclosure "in an effective and timely manner", through IIPs. The one-hour figure comes from **ACER Guidance 6.1, section 4.3, para. (133), second bullet**. It applies to inside information that does not have to be published under Reg. 2019/943, Reg. 715/2009 or Reg. 543/2013. ACER says such information should normally be published as soon as possible and at the latest within one hour, unless other applicable rules specify otherwise. Both bullets add that inside information must in any case be published before trading on it or recommending trading. Para. (134) adds ACER's expectation of a compliance plan towards real-time or near-real-time disclosure. So `binding: guidance` is right for the one-hour target. The "timely" duty is binding law. **(b) REMIT-STR-01: is there a basis for voluntary notification by MPs that are not PPATs or PPETs?**
There is no **legal** basis. Art. 15(1) and (2) are addressed only to PPATs and PPETs, and REMIT has no general reporting provision for other MPs. ACER does, however, offer a **non-binding** channel. **ACER Q&A 2.4.6** says that a member of the general public who becomes aware of potential market abuse can report it via the STR form on ACER's Notification Platform (acer-remit.eu/np/str) or through the NRA's channels. **ACER Q&A 2.4.50** separately urges MPs to report RRM non-compliance, and advises them to inform the NRA(s) and ACER of problems with their own Art. 8 reporting, via the same STR form. (G6.1 para. (434) invites reports of other breaches, but only from PPAETs.) The note also needs a scoping correction. ACER classes **DEA providers and order book providers** as PPATs (G6.1 9.2.1, paras. (416) and (418), based on Recitals 12 and 18 of Reg. 2024/1106), so some MPs are in scope.
Proposed note: *"Not a legal obligation for a market participant that is neither a PPAT nor a PPET. Note that a market participant providing direct electronic access, or acting as an order book provider, is treated by ACER as a PPAT and is in scope (ACER Guidance 6.1, section 9.2.1). Any person, including a market participant, may voluntarily report suspected market abuse to ACER via the STR form on the Notification Platform or to its NRA (ACER Q&A 2.4.6, non-binding)."*

**(c) REMIT-INS-02: is the "no combination with Art. 4(2)" statement sourced?**
Yes, but only in ACER guidance; it is not in the text of REMIT. **ACER Guidance 6.1, section 4.4, para. (136)** says that the application of Art. 4(2) cannot coincide with the application of Art. 3(4)(b), because Art. 4(2) requires that the MP makes no trading decisions based on the inside information. **Section 5.5.2, para. (217)** says the same from the side of the exemption. The reasoning follows from the wording of Art. 4(2) (the condition that the MP "does not make decisions relating to trading"), so it is a strong interpretation. Still, the check should be marked as an ACER interpretation inside a `binding: law` item.
